# Repeat value, small teams and expansion after 1.4.1

Checked **6 November 2026, Europe/Moscow**. Research only; no installations, live evaluations, outreach or product changes. The [product vision](../../PRODUCT_VISION.md), [addendum](../../PRODUCT_VISION_ADDENDUM.md) and [effort map](../../../.scratch/product-strategy-20261106/map.md) govern the baseline. Recommendations are hypotheses, not approved work.

## Decision

Optimize for **being chosen at the next relevant change**, rather than manufacturing weekly use. A developer can receive lasting value from setup and have no reason to rerun it for months. Plausible return events are an agent/version switch, changed project commands, a customized component update, or applying accepted preferences to another suitable repository. None establishes a measured return rate.

The first strategic test is whether existing `tibicen-adopt` makes such a change easier than a native audit plus ordinary editing. The second is whether one team's accepted baseline transfers to another project without erasing necessary differences. A portable manifest or continuous drift product should follow a demonstrated residual problem, rather than provide a reason to return.

## Starting point and evidence

Published **Tibicen 1.4.1** already includes task-aware init/adopt, project memory, version-bound practice evidence, bounded local MCP diagnosis, preservation, presets and portable delivery. Its project manifest records actual state; presets carry preferences with a fresh applicability check. The portable desired-composition manifest in addendum §8 remains a separate future extension. Recommending these existing features as new work would duplicate the baseline. [Release](../../evals/results/2026-11-06-release-1.4.1/README.md), [installation](../../INSTALLATION.md), [presets](../../PRODUCT_VISION.md#9-presets-and-the-page).

The concurrent [native first-use effort](../../../.scratch/native-first-use/map.md) records a passed recommendation, separate operator application and a failed fresh task; correction/read phases were unexecuted. Its underlying failed operation remains unknown. Repeat usefulness cannot be inferred from delivery checks or that incomplete cycle. Human benefit, repeat use, demand and team budget remain unmeasured. The [segments](segments.md), [competition](competition.md) and [distribution](distribution.md) studies inform this lane without determining its conclusion.

Three firsthand sources support investigating maintenance, with contrary evidence retained:

- A [WebStorm/Angular team question](https://www.reddit.com/r/angular/comments/1tgtide/is_there_a_way_to_share_and_centrally_maintain_an/) reports manually repeating instruction changes across repositories. One respondent reports scripts, an `.ai` source directory and harness links working for a very small team. Another suggests automated merge requests; this is advice, not an observed successful deployment. The post says `agent.md`, with no agent version: do not silently upgrade it to a standards-compliance incident.
- [Skills #455](https://github.com/vercel-labs/skills/issues/455), opened **1 March 2026**, reports lost local edits/helper files after updating installed skills. The **9 August** confirmation on **1.5.22** narrows the claim: edits survive while upstream is unchanged; actual replacement loses them. The issue is **open**, with a **5 November** maintainer label but no posted fix. This is specific preservation pain, not proof that every current update destroys files or that users want Tibicen.
- In the [instruction-sprawl discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeAI/comments/1rq1o1p/is_anyone_else_struggling_to_manage_claudemd/), the author is also proposing PromptVault. Replies describe Git, workspace instructions and dotfiles links; another developer reports no reusable overlap because project failure modes differ. Promotion and agreement are not independent market validation. Deliberate differences are counterevidence to universal normalization.

The strongest countercase: a team commits a concise common file, keeps exceptions local, and uses its agent's doctor when something changes. ## Ranked opportunities and falsification rules

All thresholds below are proposed small-study decision rules, not measured demand or industry benchmarks. Each experiment needs its own finite execution scope. Under [PILOT](../../PILOT.md), the owner is unavailable for assessment/recruitment; no participant, coordinator or recruiting obligation is assumed. Without willing participants, keep human outcomes unknown and limit work to preparation/source analysis.

| Rank | Acceptance trigger | Smallest experiment | Stop rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Next: event-triggered adopt revisit** | A previously useful setup encounters a real, named change and has a concrete next task. | Three consenting maintainers compare existing adopt with their strongest native/manual remedy on equivalent saved starting states; include installation, review and fresh-task effort. Observe one eligible event each, without scheduled reminders. | Stop expansion if native completes all three with equal/lower effort; stop immediately for lost accepted decisions. Unavailable events/runs are inconclusive. |
| **Next, only after an observed explanation gap: version-impact assessment** | Existing version-bound evidence/report cannot answer which local decisions an actual upgrade affects. | Manually produce three short assessments using current report data: changed upstream fact → affected local path/decision → action or justified no-change → available check. Include an irrelevant upstream release as a negative case. Compare ordinary release-note reading. | Do not add a report feature if existing output suffices, irrelevant releases cause edits, or two of three assessments offer no concrete effort/decision advantage. No new evidence engine is presumed. |
| **Later: reusable small-team baseline** | One named team repeatedly transfers an accepted common subset to at least two suitable repositories; differences are explicit. | Start with existing preset, Git-shared instructions/skills and a one-page ownership note. Apply one baseline change to two project copies plus a third incompatible copy; compare the team's ordinary script/APM route. | Stop if existing sharing is sufficient, the common subset is trivial, or preserving exceptions costs as much as manual maintenance. Require preserved exceptions and correct refusal in the incompatible copy. |
| **Later: read-only drift report; CI later still** | Maintainers repeatedly discover the same objective mismatch late, and native doctor/APM audit demonstrably miss it or are inapplicable. | First hand-author reports for six saved diffs: three objective defects and three intended deviations. Ask whether each produces an actionable decision. Only then consider a read-only local prototype before CI. | Any false alarm on an intended deviation blocks CI; detecting only native/APM findings stops duplication. Semantic advice without reliable evidence remains an interactive adopt task. |
| **Never unless a residual composition problem is observed: portable setup manifest** | Two projects need pinned composition/applicability/deviations that preset + Git + native sharing + APM cannot express adequately. | Compare three handwritten mappings: existing inputs, APM input, and proposed additional fields. No schema or installer implementation. | Reject if another dependency list or ownership layer is the main output, or authoring/migration work exceeds avoided work. This expands the current product contract. |

“Never unless” is a default exclusion, not a claim that future evidence is impossible. Do not combine these five tests into one large evaluation program. Establish first useful native completion separately; then choose one ready question. An incomplete native route is not permission for unbounded adapter work.

## Measuring repeat value and considering broader futures

Use an **opportunity-adjusted return** view: among consenting previous successful users with a documented relevant event during the observation window, how many independently choose Tibicen, start, and finish the next useful task? Report each numerator/denominator and elapsed operator effort. Separate unprompted return from study-induced use, same-project maintenance from a second repository, and justified no-change from a repair. Record all observed starts, failed/incomplete outcomes, unknown follow-up, and people with no observed event. Do not classify “no event” as churn or silently remove unknowns. Small cohorts do not establish population retention.

This supplements rather than rewrites the pilot's separate 30-day second-project metric. Do not add reminders, telemetry, a daemon or subscription merely to increase visits. A reliable rare-use utility can be a good outcome; repeat use that adds review work can be a bad one.

Broader options remain open: a **local PR maintenance assistant** becomes plausible if objective drift reports repeatedly prevent costly mistakes; an **interoperability/applicability advisor** if team baselines fail specifically at host differences; a **curated evidence/repair reference** if users benefit from guidance but prefer native execution. These are scope decisions with new maintenance obligations, not automatic extensions. Fleet enforcement, hosted synchronization and general agent orchestration are pivots; require a named buyer/problem and explicit operational contract before reconsideration. None of the inspected cases establishes a team purchasing budget.
